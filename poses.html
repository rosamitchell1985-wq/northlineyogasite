Easy Yoga Poses for Seniors — Step by Step

Practice these gentle poses at your own pace. Every pose includes a seated or supported option. Move slowly, breathe deeply, and never push into pain.

Chair PoseBeginner

1. Seated Mountain with Leg Extension

Good for: Leg strength, circulation, and knee mobility.

  1. Sit tall on a sturdy chair, feet flat on the floor, hands resting on your thighs.
  2. Lengthen your spine and relax your shoulders away from your ears.
  3. Extend one leg forward until the knee is straight, foot flexed.
  4. Hold for 3 slow breaths, then lower. Repeat 5 times per leg.
Safety tip: Keep one hand on the chair arm for support if you feel unsteady, and stop if you feel knee pain.
StandingBeginner–Intermediate

2. Supported Tree Pose

Good for: Balance, hip flexibility, and focus.

  1. Stand beside a wall or counter and rest one hand on it for support.
  2. Shift your weight onto one foot and turn the other knee outward.
  3. Place the sole of that foot against your standing leg's calf or ankle — never the knee.
  4. Bring palms together at your chest. Hold for 3–5 breaths, then switch sides.
Safety tip: Always keep one hand on support. Even touching a fingertip to the wall dramatically reduces fall risk.
FloorGentle

3. Gentle Cobra (Baby Cobra)

Good for: Back strength, posture, and easing a stiff spine.

  1. Lie on your stomach with palms on the floor under your shoulders.
  2. Keep elbows hugged close to your ribs and legs resting on the mat.
  3. Press gently through your hands to lift your head and chest a few inches.
  4. Hold for 2–3 breaths, then slowly lower. Repeat 3 times.
Safety tip: Lift only a little — this is about gentle extension, not a deep backbend. Skip this pose if you have recent spine surgery.
SeatedIntermediate

4. Half Boat Pose

Good for: Core strength and hip flexibility.

  1. Sit on the edge of a firm chair or mat with a tall spine.
  2. Hold the sides of the seat and lean back slightly, keeping the chest lifted.
  3. Lift one foot a few inches off the floor, then lower it slowly.
  4. Alternate legs for 6–8 slow repetitions.
Safety tip: Choose the chair version if getting to the floor is difficult. Keep movements small and controlled.
Chair PoseBeginner

5. Chair-Supported Tree

Good for: Practicing balance with maximum safety.

  1. Stand behind a sturdy chair and rest both hands on its back.
  2. Place one foot against the opposite ankle, keeping toes on the floor.
  3. Stand tall and breathe steadily for 3–5 breaths.
  4. Switch sides. As you improve, try using just one hand.
Safety tip: Make sure the chair is against a wall so it cannot slide away from you.
SeatedBeginner

6. Easy Seated Pose with Breathwork

Good for: Calming the mind, lowering blood pressure, and hip opening.

  1. Sit comfortably on a chair or cushion, spine tall, hands on your knees or at your heart.
  2. Close your eyes gently and breathe in through your nose for a count of 4.
  3. Exhale slowly for a count of 6. Repeat for 10 breaths.
  4. Notice your body relaxing with each exhale.
Safety tip: If sitting cross-legged is uncomfortable, keep both feet flat on the floor — the benefits are the same.
RestorativeAdvanced

7. Legs-Up-the-Wall (Viparita Karani)

Good for: Swelling in the legs, circulation, and deep relaxation.

  1. Sit with one hip close to a wall, then gently swing your legs up as you lie back.
  2. Rest your legs against the wall and relax your arms at your sides.
  3. Close your eyes and breathe slowly for 5–10 minutes.
  4. To exit, bend your knees, roll to one side, and rise slowly.
Safety tip: Skip this pose if you have uncontrolled high blood pressure or glaucoma unless your doctor approves.
StandingAll Levels

8. Standing Tall with Arms Overhead

Good for: Shoulder mobility, posture, and confidence.

  1. Stand with feet hip-width apart near a wall or counter.
  2. Inhale and sweep both arms overhead, palms facing each other.
  3. Lengthen through your fingertips and gently lift your chest.
  4. Hold for 3 breaths, then lower slowly. Repeat 3 times.
Safety tip: If raising both arms is uncomfortable, raise one arm at a time or keep hands on your hips.
